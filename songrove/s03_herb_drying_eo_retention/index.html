S03 · Herb drying — the joint optimum of harvest stage, drying temperature and energy for essential-oil quality

What is the joint optimum of harvest stage, drying temperature and energy use for essential-oil yield and chemotype quality?

Synthetic case study. Simulated data, not field observations. Seed 260203.

Headline: Optimal drying temperatures recovered for all three herbs (oregano 39 °C, sage 45 °C, peppermint 38 °C); a shared 39 °C setpoint costs at most 4% quality, while running hot (55 °C) costs peppermint 68%.

  • Truth recovered 4/4
  • Plausibility checks 4/4

Findings

  • Quality-optimal drying temperatures: oregano 38.5 °C (38.1–38.9); sage 45.3 °C (44.6–45.8); peppermint 38.1 °C (37.9–38.4).
  • Peppermint loses 68% of quality at 55 °C vs its optimum, consistent with the 'no drying above 45 °C' guidance; sage loses only 11%.
  • Accepting 5% quality loss to save energy: oregano at 42 °C uses 3.86 vs 4.13 kWh/kg (7% less); sage at 52 °C uses 3.50 vs 3.68 kWh/kg (5% less); peppermint at 42 °C uses 3.89 vs 4.18 kWh/kg (7% less).
  • A single shared setpoint for a mixed-herb dryer would be 39.0 °C, costing oregano 0%, sage 4%, peppermint 0% of quality. One shared dryer is fine; drifting above 45 °C is the real risk.
