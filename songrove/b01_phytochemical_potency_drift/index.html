B01 · Botanical potency drift — forecasting batch phytochemistry from growing-season climate

Can we forecast whether a batch will meet its Certificate of Analysis before it is harvested?

Synthetic case study. Simulated data, not field observations. Seed 260101.

Headline: Out-of-spec phenolic batches are forecastable before harvest (AUC 0.96 on held-out seasons), but most of that skill is species and farm (baseline AUC 0.94); drought is the climate signal worth watching.

  • Truth recovered 11/12
  • Plausibility checks 4/4

Findings

  • 9/12 effects are resolved with the published sign (Sun et al. 2022) or correctly read as null. Drought and the alkaloid N-deposition reversal are clear; the small N-deposition penalties on phenolics/terpenoids are not detectable with 12 farms.
  • A species-and-farm-only baseline already reaches AUC 0.94; climate adds +0.02 AUC and cuts Brier from 0.096 to 0.084.
  • The CO2 effect is not identifiable from 12 seasons: CO2 tracks the calendar (r = 1.00), so its 95% intervals are ±0.16 ln — wider than the planted effects (≤ 0.10). Separating it needs FACE/chamber data or the Sun et al. database.
  • On held-out 2023–2025 batches, a pre-harvest climate forecast ranks out-of-spec phenolics with AUC 0.96 (Brier 0.084; base rate 39%).
  • Flagging batches with forecast risk > 50% would route 43% of batches to extra testing and catch 25/28 failures.
