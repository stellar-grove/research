B10 · Intercropping overyielding under high inputs, and cover-crop legacies that flip sign by climate

Why does intercrop overyielding shrink under high inputs, and why do cover-crop legacies on the next cash crop flip sign across climates?

Synthetic case study. Simulated data, not field observations. Seed 260110.

Headline: Intercropping's land advantage erodes by 0.15 LER per 100 kg N, and cover-crop legacies range from +14% (tropical) to -11% (dry): intercrops pay most in low-input systems, cover crops where water is not limiting.

  • Truth recovered 6/7
  • Plausibility checks 3/3

Findings

  • The LER slope interval misses the planted value in this seed. Across 20 seeds the estimator is unbiased (mean −0.131 vs −0.130 per 100 kg) with 95% coverage, so this is the expected 1-in-20 miss — kept, not re-seeded.
  • Each 100 kg N/ha lowers LER by 0.15; at the average pair, overyielding falls below LER 1.2 beyond ~286 kg N/ha, so intercropping's land advantage is largest in low-input systems.
  • Cover-crop legacies flip sign by climate: +14% (tropical) to -11% (dry) for grass covers; legumes add +16%.
  • Adding soil water at sowing shrinks the climate coefficients by 70% on average (planted mediated share 60%): in dry and temperate zones the cover crop's water draw drives the penalty.
  • A legume cover in a dry climate nets +2% vs +32% in the tropics — the legume premium roughly cancels the dry-climate penalty but leaves little gain.
