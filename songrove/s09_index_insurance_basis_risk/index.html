S09 · Index insurance basis risk — how much remains, who bears it, and is the cover still worth buying?

How much basis risk remains when satellite phenology and crop models are combined, who bears it, and is the cover still worth buying?

Synthetic case study. Simulated data, not field observations. Seed 260209.

Headline: A satellite index leaves 35% of severe farm losses unpaid (crop-model hybrid 30%); the cover only beats no insurance for a CRRA-2 farmer if premium loading stays below ~27% (satellite) to ~54% (area-yield).

  • Truth recovered 3/3
  • Plausibility checks 2/2

Findings

  • With a satellite-only index (R² 0.49), 35% of farm-seasons with a severe loss get no payout; adding a crop model cuts that to 30%, area-yield crop cuts to 28%.
  • At a 20% premium loading the satellite-only contract raises certainty-equivalent income ($+1.6/farm/yr; crop model $+3.5, area-yield $+4.5). Break-even loadings: satellite 27%, crop model 42%, area-yield 54% — consistent with CMI's finding that cover helped the average farmer despite basis risk, but the margin is thin for the satellite design.
  • Basis risk never reaches zero: idiosyncratic shocks (SD 0.15) mean some severe farm losses happen in zones whose average was fine — 28% remain uncovered even with a near-perfect zone index.
