S01 · Botanical adulteration risk scoring — which lots to test, and with which method?

Given species, origin, price signal and supplier history, how likely is an incoming lot to be adulterated, and which test catches it most cheaply?

Synthetic case study. Simulated data, not field observations. Seed 260201.

Headline: A price-and-supplier risk score (AUC 0.77) plus tiered testing (Risk-tiered: HPTLC all, HPLC-MS top 25%) is the cheapest policy at $302/lot; published prevalence figures overstate a controlled supply chain.

  • Truth recovered 6/6
  • Plausibility checks 2/2

Findings

  • A 'published-study' style sample that over-selects suspicious products reports 15%–44% adulteration; the true lot-level rates are 11%–26%. Literature prevalence is an upper bound for a brand with controlled sourcing.
  • Raw screening positives run high for low-prevalence species: for turmeric HPTLC flags 14.7% of lots vs a true 11.6% (+26%); Rogan–Gladen correction recovers every species' true rate (table below).
  • A logistic risk score trained on 600 reference-tested lots ranks new lots with AUC 0.77; the top quartile by risk holds 53% of adulterated lots.
  • Lowest expected cost per lot: Risk-tiered: HPTLC all, HPLC-MS top 25% ($302/lot, 29 adulterated lots released out of 331).
