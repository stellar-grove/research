S02 · Ashwagandha label claims — how much of the withanolide gap is the analytical definition?

How far does measured withanolide content fall below label claims, and how much of that gap comes from the analytical definition ("total withanolides") rather than the raw material?

Synthetic case study. Simulated data, not field observations. Seed 260202.

Headline: Ashwagandha labels overstate HPLC withanolides ~11×, and ~94% of that gap is the assay definition, not the plant: specs should name the method, not just a percentage.

  • Truth recovered 4/4
  • Plausibility checks 3/3

Findings

  • The median product claims 5.0% but contains 0.49% by HPLC — a 11× gap.
  • 94% of the (log) gap is the analytical definition: the 'total withanolides' method reads 9.9× the HPLC sum on the same product. The remaining 6% is label rounding and manufacturer optimism.
  • Manufacturer explains a between-brand SD of 0.31 ln-units in claim vs measured 'total' — the brand you buy matters roughly 1.4× (1 SD).
  • Within 90% of label: 52% of products pass on the 'total' method and 0% on HPLC — the same capsules are compliant or wildly mislabelled depending on which assay the spec names.
