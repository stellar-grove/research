S10 · Soil-carbon MRV for smallholders — at what group size do results-based payments become feasible?

At what aggregation size does measurement error stop making results-based soil-carbon payments infeasible, and what does calibration add?

Synthetic case study. Simulated data, not field observations. Seed 260210.

Headline: Individual smallholder soil-carbon payments are within ±20% only 19% of the time; groups of ≥2500 plots with a 60-plot reference-lab check reach 90%, and without bias calibration grouping alone never gets there.

  • Truth recovered 2/2
  • Plausibility checks 2/2

Findings

  • Paying individual plots is infeasible: a single plot's credited tonnes land within ±20% of the truth only 19% of the time.
  • Grouping averages out random error but not the 0.6 t C/ha lab drift: uncorrected, even a 2,500-plot group is within tolerance 0% of the time. A 20-plot reference-lab check adds its own noise (75% at 2,500 plots); 60 calibration plots reach 93%. At the 151-plot scale of the Kenyan study the best design manages 58%.
  • At $25/t CO2 the average plot earns ~$32/ha/yr, so a 0.6 t C/ha bias is a 40% over-payment if left uncorrected.
