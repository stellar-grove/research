Consumers: does sentiment predict spending?

Does consumer sentiment tell us where spending is headed, and why are Americans so gloomy?

Real data: FRED (University of Michigan, Census, BLS), retrieved 2026-11-21. Through October 2026.

Headline: No. Adding sentiment to a spending forecast makes its error 2.9% larger outside the pandemic months. Meanwhile sentiment has run far below what unemployment and inflation predict: -47 points today, a gap 5.3 times its usual size before 2020.

  • -2.9%Forecast improvement from sentiment, excluding the pandemic months
  • -47 ptsSentiment vs what unemployment and inflation predict, today
  • 48Michigan sentiment index, Oct 2026

Findings

  • Out of sample since 2005 (237 forecasts excluding the pandemic), sentiment worsens 3-month real retail sales forecasts by 2.9% (Diebold–Mariano t = -0.64). Including the pandemic months the change is -0.5%.
  • Sentiment mostly echoes what spending data already show; it adds little that last quarter's sales don't already tell a forecaster.
  • The vibecession: since mid-2021 sentiment has averaged -31 points against its fundamentals, bottoming at -49 in May 2026. Before 2020 the gap's standard deviation was 9 points.
  • Over 1978–2019 each point of unemployment lowered sentiment by 4.3 points and each point of inflation by 1.7. Households now react to the price *level*, not just inflation, which this model leaves out.

Method

Caveats

  • Retail sales cover goods and restaurants, not services; total real spending (PCE) is only available monthly from 2007.
  • The Michigan survey moved from phone to web interviews in 2024, which may have shifted readings and could account for part of the recent gap.
  • Partisan swings in sentiment around elections are large and unrelated to spending.
