Services: why healthcare and education keep getting pricier

Why do healthcare, education and other services keep getting more expensive while TVs and computers get cheaper?

Real data: FRED (BLS CPI and Current Employment Statistics), retrieved 2026-11-21. Monthly CPI, 2000–2026; computers from 2005.

Headline: Much as Baumol predicted. Labor-intensive services have risen 3.3% a year on average, close to wage growth of 3.3%, while goods fell 0.2% a year. Since 2005 services have become 104% more expensive relative to goods.

  • 3.5%Medical care services, average annual price rise (wages: 3.3%)
  • -4.6%Toys, average annual price change
  • +104%Services prices relative to goods since 2005

Findings

  • Services outpaced goods by 3.6 points a year (p = 0.004 across the 18 categories), and averaged +0.0 points a year against wages: services prices track the cost of the labor that produces them.
  • Goods with fast productivity growth collapsed in price: toys -4.6% a year, computers -6.2%.
  • In hours of work: only 7 of 18 categories cost more hours of the average wage now than at the start; medical care services take 6% more hours, toys 88% fewer.
  • That's the cost disease, and also why it's affordable: a society that gets far more productive at making goods can afford to spend a growing share of income on services whose productivity barely moves.
  • Overall CPI inflation averaged 2.6% a year, below wage growth of 3.3%: real wages rose, but more slowly where people spend on services.

Method

Baumol’s two-sector logic: with a common wage w and labor productivity As in services and Ag in goods, unit costs are w/A, so the relative price of services moves with relative productivity:

PsPg=w/Asw/Ag=AgAs⇒gPs−gPg=gAg−gAs

Annualized growth for each category c from its start date t0, and the change in hours of work needed to buy it at the average wage w:

gc=(Pc,TPc,t0)1/(T−t0)−1,Δhoursc=Pc,T/wTPc,t0/wt0−1

Caveats

  • CPI quality adjustment drives much of the fall in computer prices; a 2026 laptop isn't a 2000 one. The CPI detail for televisions and furniture isn't published on FRED, so those classic examples are missing.
  • 'Services' here is a judgment call; shelter and rent rise for land-scarcity reasons, not only Baumol.
  • Health prices in the CPI are out-of-pocket and insurance-adjusted; total health spending per person grew faster still, because people also use more care.
