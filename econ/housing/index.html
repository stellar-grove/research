Housing: what higher mortgage rates did to prices and affordability

How much did higher mortgage rates cool home prices and affordability, and where?

Real data: FRED (Freddie Mac PMMS, S&P Cotality Case-Shiller, Census new-home sales), retrieved 2026-11-21. Case-Shiller through August 2026.

Headline: The mortgage payment on the median new home now takes 27% of median household income, up from 22% in 2019 and 19% at the 2020 low. Rates at 7.19% (vs a 2.68% low) have slowed prices more than cut them: national prices are 2.1% over the past year, and 8 of 20 metros sit more than 2% below their peak.

  • 27%Of median income needed for the median new home's mortgage payment
  • 7.19%30-year mortgage rate, Nov 2026 (low: 2.68%)
  • -4.9%Home prices 2 years after a 1-point rate rise
  • 8 of 20Metros more than 2% below their pandemic-era peak

Findings

  • Affordability: the payment share jumped from 19% (Aug 2020) to 37% (Oct 2022) and is 27% now, above the 1990–2019 average of 26%. That average includes the early 1990s, when 9–10% rates pushed it to 37%; against the 2010s, today's homes are much harder to afford.
  • Pass-through: a 1-point rise in the mortgage rate is followed by home prices -2.6% after 12 months (95% CI -4.8 to -0.4) and -4.9% after 24 (-8.4 to -1.4). Prices are sticky: sellers with low locked-in rates hold rather than cut.
  • Where: the size of a metro's pandemic run-up says little about how much it gave back (correlation +0.21). More than 2% below peak: San Francisco, Phoenix, Seattle, Tampa, Denver, Dallas, Las Vegas, Portland. At or within 0.5% of a new high: Detroit, Minneapolis, New York, Cleveland, Chicago, Boston, Miami, Washington. San Francisco is down 5% from its peak; Washington is still at a new high.
  • Median income is published through 2025; later months extend it at its trailing 5-year growth rate (5.2% a year).

Method

Monthly payment on a loan of L=0.8P (20% down) at monthly rate r=i/1200 over 360 months, as a share of monthly income Y/12:

PTIt=Lr/(1−(1+r)−360)Yt/12

Local projections: for each horizon h=0,…,24 months, with HAC standard errors,

100(lnPt+h−lnPt−1)=αh+βhΔit+∑k=112ϕhkΔlnPt−k+∑k=13ψhkΔit−k+ut+h

where P is the Case-Shiller national index and i the 30-year mortgage rate; βh traces the response.

Caveats

  • Mortgage rates move with the economy, so the local projection is a conditional association, not a clean causal estimate; an identified monetary-policy shock would sharpen it.
  • Payment-to-income ignores taxes, insurance and down-payment saving, all of which rose too.
  • The price is the median *new* home sold (Census); existing homes, most of the market, sell for less, and the mix of what's built (size, location) shifts over time.
