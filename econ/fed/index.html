Inflation and the Fed: was policy behind the curve?

Did the Fed raise rates too late, and where does policy stand against standard rules now?

Real data: FRED (Federal Reserve, BEA, BLS, CBO, University of Michigan), retrieved 2026-11-21. Through September 2026. Neutral real rate r* = 0.5% (an assumption, as in the Fed's Monetary Policy Report).

Headline: Yes. By May 2021 the balanced-approach rule called for a funds rate more than a point above the actual one; the gap peaked at 9.1 points in March 2022, and policy stayed more than a point behind for 29 months. Today the rule says 4.57% against an actual 3.75%.

  • 9.1 ptsHow far the funds rate fell short of the rule at the worst (Mar 2022)
  • 29 monthsWith policy more than a point looser than the rule, 2021–2023
  • +0.82 ptsRule minus actual today

Findings

  • The first rate hike came in March 2022, when core PCE inflation was already 5.6% and unemployment 3.7%.
  • The conclusion doesn't hinge on r*: with r* at 0%, 0.5% or 1% the peak shortfall is 8.6, 9.1 or 9.6 points. Even the gradual inertial rule sat above the actual rate through 2021–22.
  • The Phillips curve can't explain 2021–22 by itself: core inflation beat expectations by up to 3.5 points more than the 1990–2019 relationship with unemployment predicts, consistent with supply shocks and the pandemic surge in demand for goods doing much of the work.
  • Since then the gap has closed: core PCE inflation is 3.1% and the rule and actual rate are 0.8 points apart.

Method

Policy rules, with πt core PCE inflation over 12 months, π*=2, ut* CBO’s natural rate and r* the neutral real rate:

itrule=r*+πt+0.5(πt−π*)+θ(ut*−ut),θ=1 (Taylor), θ=2 (balanced approach)
itinertial=0.85it−1inertial+0.15itbalanced

Expectations-augmented Phillips curve, fit 1990–2019 with HAC errors, using Michigan 1-year expectations πe from a year earlier:

πt−πt−12e=α+κ(ut−ut*)+εt

Caveats

  • Rules are benchmarks, not prescriptions: real-time data in 2021 showed lower inflation than today's revised figures, and the Fed's 2020 framework deliberately waited for maximum employment.
  • r* is unobserved; estimates range widely, hence the sensitivity check.
  • The Michigan survey measures household expectations, which run higher and noisier than markets' or professional forecasters'.
