Labor: did the job market get a soft landing?

Did the job market cool without a jump in unemployment?

Real data: FRED (BLS Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey, Current Population Survey), retrieved 2026-11-21. Through September 2026.

Headline: Yes, so far. Job openings fell 41% from their March 2022 peak while unemployment rose only +0.5 points to 4.2%. With the pre-2020 trade-off (each 1% fall in openings came with a 1.25% rise in unemployment), a drop that large would have pushed unemployment to about 7.2%.

  • -41%Change in the job-openings rate since its Mar 2022 peak
  • +0.5 ptsChange in the unemployment rate over the same period
  • 1.05Job openings per unemployed person (2001–2019 average: 0.56)

Findings

  • The cooling came mostly from fewer openings, not more layoffs: the openings rate fell from 7.5% to 4.4% of the labor force, a move the old trade-off would have paired with unemployment near 7.2%, not 4.2%.
  • The curve itself shifted out after 2020: at any unemployment rate, openings run about 45% higher than before (95% CI 19% to 77%). Much of the 2021–22 surge was excess demand on top of normal hiring, which is why it could drain away without layoffs.
  • Tightness has come down a long way: 1.05 openings per unemployed person (peak 2.02) and a quits rate of 2.1% (2001–2019 average 1.9%). Both remain above their pre-2020 norms, leaving room for openings to fall further.

Method

Beveridge curve, with v the openings rate and u the unemployment rate; Dpost=1 from 2021 on (the 2020 collapse months excluded), HAC standard errors:

lnvt=a+blnut+γDtpost+εt

Counterfactual unemployment: move from the openings peak to today’s openings rate along a curve with the pre-2020 slope b:

u^now=upeak(vnowvpeak)1/b

Caveats

  • The openings rate here divides by the labor force; BLS's published rate divides by employment plus openings. The shapes match; the levels differ slightly.
  • JOLTS response rates have fallen since 2020, so openings are measured with more error than before.
  • A soft landing is judged so far: unemployment tends to rise nonlinearly once openings fall below their pre-2020 norm.
