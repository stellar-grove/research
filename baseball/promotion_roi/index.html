Promotion ROI: what actually puts fans in the seats

Which promotions actually lift attendance once you account for when clubs choose to run them?

Real MLB data: MLB Stats API (schedule, attendance, promotions), 2025, 2026 regular seasons (4,833 home games; neutral-site games excluded). Promotions are as listed by each club.

Headline: Postgame concert nights draw the biggest real lift, about 21% (~6,198 fans a game), after comparing each game with the same club's other games. Raw comparisons overstate most promotions because clubs schedule them on nights that sell anyway.

  • +21%Postgame concert nights, attendance lift
  • +7%Bobblehead lift (a raw comparison says +22%)
  • 4,833Home games analysed

Findings

  • 5 of 7 promotion types show a lift whose 95% interval clears zero: postgame concert, fireworks or drone show, jersey, cap or shirt giveaway, bobblehead, other giveaway.
  • Bobbleheads: raw difference 22%, adjusted 7%. The gap is scheduling, not the giveaway: bobblehead nights cluster on weekends and summer dates.
  • Among clubs with empty seats to fill (median game under 71% of their seasonal high), lifts are generally larger, consistent with sell-outs capping what a promotion can add elsewhere.
  • Ticket offers and theme nights move the needle least on attendance: their value is more likely in group sales, community and per-cap revenue, which this data can't see.

Figures

Method

Attendance model, one row per home game g, one indicator per promotion type k:

lnAttg=∑kβkPromok,g+μc(g),t+𝐱g⊤θ+εg,liftk=eβk−1

where μc,t is a club-season fixed effect and 𝐱g holds opponent, day of week × day/night, month, temperature, rain, opening day, doubleheader and both teams’ records.

  • One row per home game; outcome is log attendance, so effects read as percentage lifts.
  • Club-season fixed effects compare each game only with the same club's other games that year, which absorbs market size, ballpark, payroll and the club's overall season.
  • Promotion categories come from each club's listed promotions (keyword-matched names plus the API's offer type). One game can count in several categories.

Caveats

  • This is attendance, not profit: item costs, sponsorship that pays for them, and per-cap spending are not in public data. A team would add those to turn lift into ROI.
  • Promotions are listed, not audited; some clubs list more than others.
  • Clubs pick promotion dates knowing things this model can't see (e.g. a rivalry series or a pre-sold group), so treat lifts as upper bounds where selection is likely.
