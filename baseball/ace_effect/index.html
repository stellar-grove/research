The ace effect: do elite starters sell tickets?

When an elite starter takes the mound, how many extra fans show up?

Real MLB data: MLB Stats API (schedule, attendance, pitching), 2022–2026 regular seasons (12,051 home games). Aces are the top 20 starters by FIP the season before (25+ starts).

Headline: Elite starters barely move the gate. A home ace moves attendance +0.0%, an interval (-1.1% to +1.2%) that includes zero; a visiting ace moves attendance +1.1%, an interval (-0.2% to +2.3%) that includes zero. Fans buy the date, the opponent and the promotion, not the probable starter.

  • +0.0%Home-ace attendance lift (95% CI -1.1% to +1.2%)
  • +1.1%Visiting-ace attendance lift
  • 1,259Home starts by aces analysed

Findings

  • 1,259 home starts by aces and 1,211 visiting starts across five seasons: plenty of data, so a narrow interval around zero is a real finding, not a lack of power.
  • Monday–Thursday home-ace lift +2.5% (+0.5% to +4.6%); Friday–Sunday -2.6% (-4.8% to -0.4%). A midweek lift is plausible (more empty seats to fill), but a weekend *penalty* is not something fans would cause; it more likely reflects how clubs line up rotations around series and off-days. Treat the split as a scheduling pattern, not a menu.
  • Individually, 4 of 16 aces with 25+ home starts have an interval that excludes zero, in both directions. With 16 estimates, one or two would do that by chance alone, so no single pitcher here is a proven draw.
  • Most tickets are sold before the rotation is set, which is why the starter barely registers. The marketing play is to announce a marquee starter early for a soft date and promote him, not to expect the matchup to sell itself.

Figures

Method

Attendance model, one row per home game g:

lnAttg=βHAceghome+βAAcegaway+μc(g),t+𝐱g⊤θ+εg

where μc,t is a club-season fixed effect and 𝐱g the game controls; the attendance lift is eβ−1.

  • Outcome: log attendance per home game, so effects read as percentages.
  • Controls: club-season fixed effects, opponent, day of week x day/night, month, temperature band, rain, opening day, doubleheader, and both teams' season-to-date records (shrunk toward .500 early on).
  • Probable starters come from the schedule; a late scratch would count against the listed starter.

Caveats

  • FIP rank is a proxy for star power. Fame (Ohtani, a rookie phenom) can outrun last year's numbers.
  • Most tickets sell before the rotation is known; the effect on walk-up and late sales is surely larger than this average across all tickets.
  • Sell-outs cap the measurable lift at the most popular parks.
