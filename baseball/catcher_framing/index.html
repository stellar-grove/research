Catcher framing: the hidden runs behind the plate

How many runs do catchers save or cost by how they receive the ball?

Real MLB data: Statcast via Baseball Savant, 2025–2026 regular seasons (356,497 taken pitches in 2026). Leaderboard: catchers with 3,000+ taken pitches in 2026.

Headline: Framing is a real, repeatable skill worth a lot of runs. Austin Wells saved his pitchers about 10 runs in 2026; Logan O'Hoppe cost his 9. The year-to-year correlation in framing rate is 0.43.

  • +10 runsAustin Wells, the top framer of 2026

Findings

  • The called-strike model separates strikes from balls with AUC 0.990: location and count explain almost every call, so what's left over is umpire noise plus the catcher.
  • Each extra called strike is worth about 0.142 runs on average, more in hitter's counts (priced count by count from Statcast run values).
  • Among regulars, the 90th-percentile framer is about 12 runs better than the 10th: comparable to the gap between an average and a good everyday hitter.
  • It's a skill, not luck: framing rate correlates 0.43 from 2025 to 2026 (47 catchers) and 0.59 between odd and even games within 2026.

Figures

Method

Framing runs for catcher c, over every taken pitch i he received:

Framing runsc=∑i∈c(si−p^i)vk(i),vk=ΔREkball−ΔREkstrike

where si=1 for a called strike, p^i is the cross-fitted called-strike probability and vk is the run value of a strike instead of a ball in count k (average ΔRE after each).

  • Taken pitches only (called strikes and balls); blocked balls, pitchouts and swings excluded.
  • Location is measured against each batter's own zone (Statcast sz_bot and sz_top), so tall and short hitters are comparable.
  • Cross-fitting by game keeps the model from memorising the very calls it's grading.

Caveats

  • Umpire identity isn't in public Statcast data; over a season a catcher sees many umpires, which averages out, but single games are noisy.
  • The pitcher's command and the pitch type also shape calls; a fuller model would add pitch type and movement and pitcher effects.
  • Framing value shrinks if the automated ball-strike challenge system arrives in MLB.
