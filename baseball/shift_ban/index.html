After the shift ban: who gained, and did it last?

Did banning the infield shift help the hitters it targeted, and did the effect last?

Real MLB data: Statcast via Baseball Savant, 2022, 2023, 2025, 2026 regular seasons. 60 heavily shifted and 121 rarely shifted hitters in the 2022→2023 comparison.

Headline: Banning the shift raised formerly shifted hitters' hit rate on grounders and low liners by about +36 points in 2023 relative to everyone else (95% CI +21 to +51): roughly 5 extra hits a season. By 2026 the edge is +28 points as defenses adapted with legal shades.

  • +36 ptsGround-ball hit-rate gain for formerly shifted hitters, 2023
  • +28 ptsGain that remained in 2026
  • 5 hitsExtra hits a season for a typical shifted regular

Findings

  • In 2022, a third of balls in play faced an illegal-by-2023 infield shift. The ban didn't end positioning: legal 'shades' and strategic alignments now cover 37% of balls in play.
  • The gain is net of the 2023 changes that helped everyone (bigger bases, pitch clock), because the rarely shifted group absorbs them, and net of contact quality (exit velocity and launch angle bins).
  • At ~0.75 runs per hit converted from an out, 5 hits is worth about 4 runs a season for a typical formerly shifted regular: real, but smaller than the pre-ban rhetoric.

Figures

Method

Difference-in-differences on each low ball in play j by hitter i:

Hitij=αi+γt(j)+β(Shiftedi×Postt(j))+𝐳j⊤δ+εij

where αi and γt are hitter and season fixed effects, 𝐳j holds exit-velocity and launch-angle bins, and β is the net gain from the ban.

  • Balls in play are those with a Statcast BABIP value (home runs excluded); 'low' = ground balls plus line drives under 15°.
  • Treatment is set by 2022 shift exposure only, so it can't be influenced by post-ban results.

Caveats

  • Hitters who were shifted were different hitters (mostly left-handed pull hitters); fixed effects absorb stable differences, not changes in approach after the ban.
  • 2024 isn't included; the trend through 2025–2026 shows durability.
  • The runs conversion uses a round 0.75 runs per hit; a team would use its own linear weights.
