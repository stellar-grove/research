Baserunning under the new rules: when to send the runner

With bigger bases and pickoff limits, when should a runner go?

Real MLB data: MLB Stats API; Statcast and Baseball Savant leaderboards. League totals 2021–2026; run expectancy and runner-level data from 2026.

Headline: The 2023 rules turned the running game back on: attempts jumped from 1.36 to 1.88 a game by 2024 and success from 75% to 80%. At 77% in 2026, league runners still beat the 65%–74% break-even for the most common steal (second base, 0–1 outs).

  • 1.88Steal attempts per game at the 2024 peak (2022: 1.36)
  • 77%Stolen-base success in 2026
  • 74%Break-even for stealing second with one out

Findings

  • Leads barely moved (primary 11.6 → 11.7 ft; secondary 15.2 → 15.2 ft) and pickoff outs stay rare (0.4 → 0.7 per 1,000 running opportunities). So the jump in success isn't runners taking more ground; it's consistent with bigger bases and with pitchers limited to two disengagements per plate appearance.
  • Since 2024 attempts have eased and success has slipped to 77%: consistent with defenses adapting (quicker deliveries, catchers) and with the extra attempts being lower-percentage ones.
  • Speed drives *who* runs far more than *how often they make it*: attempt rate climbs from 0.3% for the slowest band to 3.1% for 29+ ft/s, while success stays in a narrow 72%–82% range. Slower runners mostly go in their best spots. The exception: the 26–27 ft/s band succeeds 72% of the time, below the one-out break-even, so those runners should go less often, not more.
  • Break-evens (from 2026 run expectancy) range from 65% to 84%: stealing third with two outs needs near-certainty; stealing second with nobody out is the cheapest gamble.

Figures

Method

Break-even success rate for a steal attempt, from the run-expectancy table RE:

p*=RE(before)−RE(caught)RE(safe)−RE(caught)

where RE(b,o) is the average number of runs scored from base state b with o outs to the end of the half-inning. A runner should go when his chance of success exceeds p*.

  • RE24 = average runs from each base-out state to the end of the half-inning, innings 1–8 of the 2026 season.
  • Pickoffs, leads, attempts and opportunities come from Savant's basestealing run value leaderboard.

Caveats

  • Catcher pop time and pitcher time-to-plate matter for each attempt; this runner-level view averages over them. An attempt-level model would add both.
  • Runners who attempt are selected (they pick good counts and slow pitchers), so a slow runner's success rate says little about what would happen if he ran more.
  • Break-even ignores the hitter at the plate and the inning/score context that a dugout would use.
