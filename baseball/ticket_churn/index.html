Season-ticket churn: who won't renew, and who's worth an offer

Which season-ticket holders won't renew, and where does a retention offer actually pay?

SYNTHETIC — simulated data, not a real club. 15,000 season-ticket accounts with planted drivers of non-renewal and a randomized retention-offer pilot. Seed 20261101.

Headline: Ticket usage is the tell: every 10 points of scan rate lost raises the odds of leaving by about 27%. But the accounts most likely to leave aren't the best ones to spend offers on: targeting by expected net value instead of raw churn risk turns 2,000 offers from $5.27M into $10.75M of saved lifetime value.

  • $10.75MNet value of 2,000 offers, targeted by value
  • $5.27MSame budget, targeted by churn risk
  • +27%Higher odds of leaving per 10-point drop in ticket usage

Findings

  • The churn model's holdout AUC is 0.70, against 0.70 for the planted true probabilities: renewal is genuinely noisy, and the model gets nearly all the signal there is. It's also calibrated decile by decile, so its probabilities can be used as probabilities in budgeting.
  • Biggest planted drivers recovered: scan rate, resale rate (accounts reselling most of their seats are brokers or drifting fans), first-year status, partial plans and price.
  • Offers work where engagement is slipping: the pilot cut churn sharply for low-usage accounts and barely moved engaged ones, which is why the response model, not the risk model, should pick targets.
  • Risk-targeting saves more accounts (269 vs 239) but value-targeting saves the ones worth keeping: $46,177 of lifetime value per saved account vs $20,682. Random offers return $2.58M.

Method

Baseline risk, uplift and lifetime value for account i:

logitPr(churni)=𝐱i⊤β
uplifti=p^i(no offer)−p^i(offer),CLVi=Ri∑t=09(1−p^i1.08)t
net valuei=uplifti·CLVi−c

where Ri is annual ticket plus in-park revenue, the horizon is 10 years at 8% discount, and c = $150 per offer.

  • Churn model: logistic regression on the pilot's no-offer accounts (baseline risk), 70/30 train/holdout split; calibration shown for no-offer holdout accounts.
  • Uplift: the same model with offer × feature interactions; an account's uplift is its predicted churn without an offer minus with one.

Caveats

  • Real accounts have richer signals: secondary-market pricing, service tickets, seat relocations, household changes. A club would add them.
  • The pilot is what makes uplift estimable. Without a randomized holdout, offer response can't be separated from who got offers.
  • Team performance moves everyone's renewal at once; with one season it's in the baseline, not the model.
