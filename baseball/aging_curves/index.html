Aging curves: what a free agent's bat is worth over a five-year deal

How fast do hitters age, and what does that mean for a five-year free-agent contract?

Real MLB data: Baseball Savant expected stats; MLB Stats API (birth dates), 2021–2026 (non-pitchers). Dollar values use an assumed $9M per WAR.

Headline: Hitters peak around 24–25 and decline steadily after. The same .360 bat is worth about 17.1 WAR over five years if signed at 27, 15.6 at 30 and 14.2 at 33.

  • 24Peak age for hitters
  • 59 ptswOBA lost from peak by age 33
  • 2.9 WARCost of signing the same bat at 33 instead of 27

Findings

  • Survivors-only, the curve peaks at 25; corrected, at 24. With a 15-point phantom penalty the correction is modest: it trims about 0.1 WAR off a 33-year-old's five years. It bites hardest in the late 30s, where few players survive to be measured, and a harsher penalty would steepen it.
  • From peak, the corrected curve loses about 59 points of wOBA by 33 and 83 by 35.
  • Same .360 bat, different ages: five-year value of age 27 ≈ $154M, age 30 ≈ $140M, age 33 ≈ $128M. The age gap, not the correction, is the big number: six years of age costs about 2.9 WAR over the same five-year deal.

Figures

Method

Average change in wOBA from age a to a+1, over players i with seasons at both ages:

Δa=∑iwi(wOBAi,a+1−wOBAi,a)∑iwi,wi=21/PAi,a+1/PAi,a+1

Projected wins above replacement from batting, per 600 plate appearances:

WAR=110(wOBA−wOBAlg1.2×600+20)

where wOBAlg is the league average, 1.2 converts wOBA to runs per PA, 20 runs is replacement level and 10 runs make a win.

  • Pairs: 200+ PA in both seasons; weight = harmonic mean of the two PA totals.
  • Phantom seasons: players with 200+ PA who fall under 100 the next year get a next season at their wOBA − 15 points, at half weight. This is an assumption, and the page shows the uncorrected curve alongside it.
  • Age-to-age changes are smoothed over three adjacent ages before chaining; ages past the last one with 15+ pairs reuse the final change.

Caveats

  • Six seasons is a short window; a team model would use decades and separate skills (power, contact, speed) that age differently.
  • Batting only. Defense and speed decline earlier than hitting, so full-WAR curves fall faster.
  • $9M per WAR is a round market price, not a law: plug in your own.
