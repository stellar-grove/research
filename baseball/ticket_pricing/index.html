Dynamic ticket pricing: what each seat should cost, game by game

How should a club price each section, game by game, and how do you learn what fans will pay?

SYNTHETIC — simulated data, not a real club. A fictional mid-market club, 3 seasons × 81 home games × 6 sections; demand drivers sized from the real attendance studies; elasticities planted. Seed 20260611.

Headline: In this simulated club, pricing each section to its measured price sensitivity lifts game-day revenue +25.4% over current pricing, mostly by filling empty seats (the park runs 61% full today). Using naive elasticities instead changes revenue by -1.7%: getting the estimate right is the whole game.

Findings

  • Naive price-vs-sales data is upside down: because busy nights are priced up, it shows elasticities near zero or even positive. A season of randomized ±10% price tests on 20% of section-games recovers the planted values (6 of 6 within the 95% interval).
  • Prices go up in: Premium club. Prices come down in: Field box, Infield reserve, Outfield, Bleachers, Upper deck. Even fairly inelastic sections get cheaper here, because each extra fan brings $28 of in-park spending on top of the ticket.
  • The size of the prize depends on that per-cap and on how many seats sit empty: ticket revenue alone, the uplift is +12.0%; at $28 per fan it's +25.4%. A club with a full park would see much less.
  • One season of price tests is enough: optimising on the estimated elasticities lands within 0.0% of what perfect knowledge of the planted values would deliver (all within the 70%–150%-of-list fairness guardrails).

Method

Demand for section s at game g, identified by the randomized price tests:

lnQsg=αg+δs+εslnPsg+usg

Optimal price, within the fairness guardrails around list price Ls and capacity Ks:

Psg*=argmax0.7Ls≤P≤1.5Ls(P+π)·min{Ks,Q^sg(P)}

where εs is the section’s price elasticity, αg a game fixed effect and π = $28 of in-park spend per fan.

  • Identified model: log tickets ~ section × log price + game fixed effects + section effects, on section-games that didn't sell out (sell-outs hide true demand). With game fixed effects, the only price variation left is the randomized tests.
  • Optimiser: per section-game grid search over 70–150% of list, maximising ticket revenue plus $28 in-park spend per fan, with the club's demand forecast as input.
  • The backtest scores every pricing scenario with the true (planted) demand model, which only a simulation can do.

Caveats

  • Real demand has more structure: secondary-market prices, season-ticket holders' seats, group sales, and fans reacting to price changes over time (reference prices), all of which a club would model.
  • The in-park spend per fan is an assumption; with your own per-cap data the optimum shifts.
  • Constant elasticity within the guardrails is a simplification.
