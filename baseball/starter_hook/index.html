When to pull the starter

At what point does a tiring starter become worse than the bullpen alternative?

Real MLB data: Statcast via Baseball Savant, 2026 regular season (102,961 plate appearances against starters, 78,622 against relievers).

Headline: The same starter allows about +17 points more wOBA the third time through the order than the first, after adjusting for who's batting. Leaving a middle starter in for a third look instead of going to an average reliever costs about 0.14 runs per trip through the lineup. Even an ace's third look is outpitched by a top-third reliever (+0.11 runs per trip), though he still beats the average one.

Findings

  • Within-pitcher penalty: 2nd time through +14 points (95% CI ±7), 3rd time +17 (±9). Comparing each pitcher with himself matters: better starters go deeper, so a pooled comparison understates the penalty.
  • Yardsticks (batter-adjusted wOBA allowed): average reliever .314, top-third reliever .290; first time through, a top-third starter is .288, a middle starter .316, a bottom-third starter .339.
  • Inning for inning, an average reliever is about as good as a middle starter's first look at a lineup. The starter's value is volume: every out he gets saves bullpen innings for the rest of the series. The table below prices that trade-off.
  • Fatigue shows up in the radar gun: four-seam velocity is down about 0.6 mph by pitches 75–89. Past 90 pitches the sample is survivors (starters managers trusted), so those bands flatter the starter.

Method

Each plate appearance p is adjusted for the batter, then the penalty is estimated within pitcher:

adjp=wOBAp−q^b(p)+wOBAlg,adjp=αpitcher(p)+τTTO(p)+εp

Runs cost of staying with a starter of quality qSP instead of a reliever of quality qRP, per trip through the lineup:

cost=91.2(qSP+τTTO−qRP)

where q^b is the batter’s leave-one-out season wOBA shrunk toward league, τTTO the times-through penalty, and 1.2 converts wOBA to runs.

  • Starter = the first pitcher of each team's half of the game. PAs with a wOBA denominator only.
  • Batter adjustment: each batter's leave-one-out season wOBA, shrunk toward league with 200 PA.
  • The times-through penalty comes from a regression with pitcher fixed effects, errors clustered by game.
  • Starter tiers use first-time-through quality; relievers use all relief PAs (100+), both shrunk with 150 PA, so the two are measured the same way.

Caveats

  • Managers already pull struggling starters, so the third-time-through sample leans toward pitchers who were going well: the true penalty is, if anything, larger.
  • Relievers aren't free: bullpen fatigue across a series and roster limits decide how often you can use a top-third arm. A team would layer that on.
  • Lineup slot matters: the third time through usually starts at the top of the order.
