Why small populations die out

If births outnumber deaths, how can a population go extinct, and how many founders make it safe?

Chapter 5 · Birth and death: probabilistic dynamics. A worked example rebuilt from the chapter's topic in Mike Mesterton-Gibbons, *A Concrete Approach to Mathematical Modelling*; the scenario and numbers are our own illustration, not the book's text.

Headline: With 25% more births than deaths, a colony of 5 still dies out with probability 33%. Getting that risk under 5% takes 14 founders.

  • 33%chance a 5-animal colony eventually dies out
  • 14founders needed for a 95% chance of establishment
  • 28%already extinct within 10 years

The problem

Conservationists release 5 animals into a new reserve. Each animal gives birth at rate λ=1.0 a year and dies at rate μ=0.8. The deterministic model dN/dt=(λ−μ)N says the colony grows by 20% a year, forever. Is it safe?

The model

Treat births and deaths as random events. With n animals, the next event comes after an exponential waiting time with rate (λ+μ)n and is a birth with probability λ/(λ+μ). The probabilities pn(t) obey the forward (master) equations:

dpndt=λ(n−1)pn−1+μ(n+1)pn+1−(λ+μ)npn

The mean still follows the deterministic curve, EN(t)=n0e(λ−μ)t, but zero is an absorbing state. Each founder's line of descendants dies out independently, with probability μ/λ (the smallest root of μ−(λ+μ)s+λs2=0), so:

P(extinction)=(μλ)n0

For a risk below 5%, solve (μ/λ)n0≤0.05:

n0≥ln0.05ln(μ/λ)

Here (0.8)5=0.328, and n0≥13.4, so 14 founders.

Key findings

  • Growth on average is not safety. The expected colony grows 20% a year, yet 33% of colonies of 5 vanish, most of them early: 28% are gone within 10 years.
  • Risk falls geometrically with founders: each extra animal multiplies it by μ/λ=0.8. 14 founders bring it below 5%; doubling the margin λ−μ cuts the number needed far more.
  • Simulation agrees with the formula. In 4,000 simulated colonies, 33.6% were extinct by year 60 against 32.8% predicted (±1.5%).

Assumptions and extensions

  • Linear rates ignore crowding; at large sizes add a carrying capacity (state-dependent rates go straight into birth_death.gillespie and forward_equations).
  • No catastrophes or Allee effects. Fires, disease, or difficulty finding mates at low density all raise the risk above this baseline.
  • Demographic noise only. Environmental noise (good and bad years for everyone at once) does not average out with more founders.
