Stellar Grove · Concrete Mathematical Modelling

From order to chaos in one line

A population that breeds once a year follows x → r x (1 − x). How can such a simple rule be unpredictable?

Chapter 10 · Further dynamical systems. A worked example rebuilt from the chapter's topic in Mike Mesterton-Gibbons, *A Concrete Approach to Mathematical Modelling*; the scenario and numbers are our own illustration, not the book's text.

Headline: As the growth rate r rises the population goes from steady, to alternating every other year at r = 3, to 4-, 8-, 16-year cycles, and into chaos past r ≈ 3.5700. At r = 3.9 two populations differing by one part in 100 million disagree completely within 29 years.

  • 3.5700where period doubling accumulates and chaos begins
  • 4.67ratio of successive doubling gaps (Feigenbaum's δ = 4.669)
  • 29 yrsfor a 10⁻⁸ difference to grow to 0.1 at r = 3.9

The problem

Insects with one generation a year have no overlap between generations, so their dynamics are a map from one year to the next rather than a differential equation. The simplest map with crowding is the discrete logistic. What long-run behaviour does it produce?

The model

xn+1=rxn(1−xn),0≤xn≤1,0<r≤4

The fixed point x*=1−1/r is stable while |f′(x*)|=|2−r|<1, i.e. for 1<r<3. At r=3 the derivative passes through −1 and a 2-cycle is born (a period-doubling bifurcation); it loses stability in turn at r=1+6=3.4495, and so on. The gaps between doublings shrink geometrically:

limk→∞rk−rk−1rk+1−rk=δ=4.6692…

Beyond the accumulation point r∞≈3.5699 most orbits are chaotic. The test is the Lyapunov exponent, the average stretching rate along the orbit:

λ=limN→∞1N∑n=1Nln|r(1−2xn)|>0 means chaos

Key findings

  • Simple rules, complex behaviour: one parameter takes a deterministic model through every kind of long-run behaviour, with no randomness anywhere.
  • Chaos means sensitive dependence: the Lyapunov exponent at r = 4 is 0.693 (exactly ln 2 in theory), so errors double each year on average; long-range forecasts are impossible even with a perfect model.
  • Order returns in windows: inside the chaotic region, r = 3.83 has a stable 3-cycle (Lyapunov -0.370).
  • Universality: the gap ratios 4.751, 4.656, 4.668 approach δ = 4.669, the same constant for every smooth one-hump map.

Assumptions and extensions

  • Real insects rarely sit in the chaotic range: fitted r values for most field populations give stable points or short cycles (Hassell, Lawton and May, 1976).
  • Noise blurs the picture: with chance added, cycles and chaos are hard to tell apart from short series.
  • Continuous-time systems need at least three variables for chaos (Lorenz); maps can do it with one.
