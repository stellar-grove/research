Dimensional analysis

Before measuring anything, what form must the law for a pendulum's period take?

Chapter 9 · Building a model: adapting, extending, and combining. A worked example rebuilt from the chapter's topic in Mike Mesterton-Gibbons, *A Concrete Approach to Mathematical Modelling*; the scenario and numbers are our own illustration, not the book's text. The pendulum timings are simulated (with 1% timing noise) so the fitted law can be checked against the exact one.

Headline: Units alone force the period of a pendulum to be T=Cl/g: mass cannot matter, and doubling the length multiplies the period by √2. The experiment then only has to find one number, Cg=6.26 (theory: 2π = 6.28).

  • 1dimensionless group for the pendulum: T²g/l
  • 0.499fitted exponent of length (dimensional analysis says ½)
  • 2groups for drag on a sphere: F/(ρv²d²) and the Reynolds number

The problem

A pendulum's period T might depend on its length l, the bob's mass m and gravity g. Rather than measure every combination, ask what the units allow.

Buckingham's Π theorem

Write each variable's dimensions in terms of mass M, length L and time T. A physical law can only relate dimensionless combinations Π=Talbgcmd. Requiring every exponent of M, L and T to vanish gives a linear system, the null space of the dimension matrix:

TlgmM0001L0110T10−20⇒a=2c,b=−c,d=0

Four variables, rank three: one group, Π=T2g/l. The law must be Π= constant, so

T=Clg

Mass dropped out because nothing else carries M to cancel it. For drag on a sphere (force F, fluid density ρ, speed v, diameter d, viscosity μ) the same computation gives 2 groups; the computed basis is F / rho v^2 d^2 and F rho / mu^2. Any products of powers of groups are also groups, and the ratio of these two is the square of the Reynolds number ρvd/μ. So the drag coefficient F/(ρv2d2) must be a function of the Reynolds number alone, which collapses a five-variable experiment onto a single curve.

The experiment

Timing 21 swings at seven lengths with bobs of 0.1, 0.5 and 2 kg and fitting T=Clp gives p=0.499 (95% interval 0.493–0.504) and C=1.998, so Cg=6.258, against the small-swing theory value 2π=6.283. The correlation between bob mass and T/l is -0.28.

Key findings

  • Units constrain laws. Without any physics of forces, dimensional analysis gives the l/g scaling and rules out mass; only the constant C needs an experiment (or the differential equation).
  • The data agree: the fitted exponent 0.499 is within sampling error of ½, and bob mass has no significant relationship with the scaled period (r = -0.28, p = 0.22).
  • Fewer variables, cheaper experiments: drag depends on five quantities, but only on one combination of them, so wind-tunnel tests on small models predict full-size behaviour at the same Reynolds number.

Assumptions and extensions

  • The list of variables must be complete. Forget one (the swing amplitude, for large swings) and the analysis silently gives a wrong law; amplitude is itself dimensionless, so it enters as a second Π.
  • Groups are not unique: any product of powers of valid groups is valid; choose the ones with physical meaning.
  • Scaling a model by its natural units (as in building.nondimensional_harvest) is the same idea applied to differential equations.
