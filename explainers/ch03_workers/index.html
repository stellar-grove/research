How many workers to hire

With its machines fixed, how many workers should a firm employ, and how does that change with the wage?

Chapter 3 · Optimal control and utility. A worked example rebuilt from the chapter's topic in Mike Mesterton-Gibbons, *A Concrete Approach to Mathematical Modelling*; the scenario and numbers are our own illustration, not the book's text.

Headline: Hire until the last worker's output is worth exactly the wage: 100 workers. A 10% pay rise cuts that to 79, a 21% drop, because labour demand has elasticity -2.5 when labour's share is 0.6.

  • 100workers that maximise profit
  • -2.5% change in hiring per 1% change in the wage
  • 60%of revenue paid as wages, whatever the wage

The problem

A workshop has K=100 machines, sells its product at p=$2 and pays w=$30 an hour. More workers raise output, but each extra worker adds less than the last (they share the same machines). How many should it hire?

The model

Output follows a Cobb-Douglas production function with constant returns to scale:

Q(L)=ALβK1−β,π(L)=pQ(L)−wL

Profit is concave in L (because β<1), so the maximum is where its derivative is zero: the value of the marginal product equals the wage.

pβALβ−1K1−β=w⟹L*=(pβAK1−βw)1/(1−β)

With the numbers above, L*=100.0, producing 2,500 units an hour for a profit of $2,000 an hour before paying for the machines.

Because L*∝w−1/(1−β), the wage elasticity of labour demand is constant:

dlnL*dlnw=−11−β

Key findings

  • The rule is marginal: the right question is never 'are workers profitable on average?' but 'does the next one bring in more than they cost?'
  • Hiring is very sensitive to pay: elasticity −1/(1−β)=−2.5. The more output depends on labour (higher β), the more sensitive (drag β toward 0.85).
  • With Cobb-Douglas technology, the wage bill is always the share β=0.6 of revenue: a higher wage means fewer workers, not a bigger share for labour.

Assumptions and extensions

  • Price-taking. A firm big enough to move its product price (or the local wage) hires fewer; the rule becomes marginal revenue product = marginal labour cost.
  • Fixed machines. In the long run capital adjusts too, and with constant returns the firm's size is set by demand, not by this rule.
  • Utility. The same Lagrange calculus gives consumer demand: control.cobb_douglas_demand and control.maximize_utility.
