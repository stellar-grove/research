Fishing to a fixed quota

How much can a fishery take each year, and what happens if the quota is set slightly too high?

Chapter 1 · Growth and decay: dynamical systems. A worked example rebuilt from the chapter's topic in Mike Mesterton-Gibbons, *A Concrete Approach to Mathematical Modelling*; the scenario and numbers are our own illustration, not the book's text.

Headline: The stock can sustain at most 12.5 thousand tonnes a year. A quota of 10 is safe while the stock stays above 28; a quota of 13, just 4% over the maximum, wipes the fishery out in 51 years.

  • 12.5k tmaximum sustainable yield rK/4, at a stock of K/2
  • 28k tcollapse threshold with a 10k t quota
  • 51 yrsfrom a healthy 75k t stock to zero at a 13k t quota

The problem

A fish stock grows logistically: fast when small, levelling off at a carrying capacity of K=100 thousand tonnes, with intrinsic growth rate r=0.5 a year. Regulators set a fixed annual catch H. Which quotas can be sustained, and what goes wrong when the quota is too big?

The model

dxdt=rx(1−xK)−H

Equilibria are where growth exactly replaces the catch, rx(1−x/K)=H, a quadratic:

x±=K2(1±1−4HrK)

The upper root is stable and the lower one is a threshold: below it, the catch outpaces growth and the stock falls to zero. The two roots merge and vanish when the square root hits zero, at the maximum sustainable yield:

HMSY=rK4atx=K2

Here HMSY=12.5. With H=10, the stock settles at x+=72.4, and x−=27.6 is the point of no return.

Key findings

  • Yield rises smoothly with the quota up to 12.5k t, then the sustainable state vanishes: there is no gentle decline, only a cliff (a saddle-node bifurcation).
  • Near the maximum the margin for error is thin: at a 10k t quota, a stock knocked below 28k t by a bad year or an overestimate collapses even though the quota is legal (from 25k t it takes 8 years).
  • A quota 4% over MSY empties a healthy 75k t stock in 51 years, slowly at first, so the warning signs arrive late.
  • Effort-based rules (catch proportional to stock, growth.effort_equilibrium) have no cliff: as the stock falls, so does the catch.

Assumptions and extensions

  • Logistic growth is the simplest shape with a maximum; real stocks often have depensation (poor breeding at low density), which makes the threshold higher.
  • Known parameters. r and K are estimated with error; setting the quota at MSY using an overestimate is how several real fisheries collapsed.
  • Chapter 3 adds economics: with discounting, the profit-maximising stock is below K/2 (see the optimal harvesting explainer).
