Cleaning up a polluted lake

Once the pollution stops, how long until the lake is safe again?

Chapter 1 · Growth and decay: dynamical systems. A worked example rebuilt from the chapter's topic in Mike Mesterton-Gibbons, *A Concrete Approach to Mathematical Modelling*; the scenario and numbers are our own illustration, not the book's text.

Headline: With the discharge stopped, the lake falls from 20 to 5 µg/L in 6.2 years. If the inflow is only cut to 6 µg/L, it never gets there.

  • 6.2 yrsto reach the 5 µg/L safe level after a full shutdown
  • 4 yrsresidence time V/F: how long water stays in the lake
  • 2.8 yrsfor the excess pollution to halve, whatever its level

The problem

A factory has been discharging into the river that feeds a lake of volume V=12 km³. River water flows in and out at F=3 km³ a year, and the lake now holds 20 µg/L of the pollutant. The factory is closed, so the inflow carries only a background 1 µg/L. How long until the lake drops below the safe level of 5 µg/L?

The model

Treat the lake as well mixed: what flows out has the lake's own concentration c(t). The amount of pollutant Vc changes by what comes in minus what goes out:

Vdcdt=Fcin−Fc

This is linear and first order. Its solution decays exponentially toward the inflow level, with rate F/V, the reciprocal of the residence time:

c(t)=cin+(c0−cin)e−Ft/V

Setting c(T)=c* and solving for T gives the clean-up time:

T=VFlnc0−cinc*−cin

With the numbers above, T=4ln(19/4)=6.23 years.

Key findings

  • Clean-up time scales with the residence time V/F=4 years: double the lake's volume or halve its flow and every clean-up takes twice as long.
  • The excess pollution halves every ln2·V/F=2.8 years, so the last stretch to the target takes as long as the first stretch of the same ratio.
  • The lake settles at the inflow level. Cutting the inflow only to 6 µg/L, above the 5 µg/L target, means the lake never becomes safe (T = ∞): the source must be cleaned, not just reduced.

Assumptions and extensions

  • Well mixed. Real lakes stratify; a layered model (two compartments) slows the clean-up of the deep water.
  • Constant flow. Seasonal flow makes F a function of time; growth.lake_rhs accepts a time-varying inflow concentration and growth.solve integrates it.
  • No decay or settling. A pollutant that also breaks down at rate k clears at rate F/V+k instead.
