The queue at a red light

Cars stop at a red light and start again at green. How long does the queue get, and how long does it take to clear?

Chapter 11 · Further flow and diffusion. A worked example rebuilt from the chapter's topic in Mike Mesterton-Gibbons, *A Concrete Approach to Mathematical Modelling*; the scenario and numbers are our own illustration, not the book's text.

Headline: During a 45 s red, the back of the queue moves upstream at 3 m/s even though every car moves forward or stands still. It keeps growing after green, peaking at 169 m, and needs 80 s of green to clear completely.

  • 169 mlongest queue (about 25 cars), 11 s after green
  • 80 sof green needed to dissolve the queue
  • 3 m/sspeed at which the queue's back moves upstream

The problem

Traffic arrives at 1,296 vehicles an hour (density 30 per km, free speed 54 km/h) at a light that is red for 45 seconds. Treating traffic as a continuous fluid, how does the queue form and dissolve?

The model (Lighthill–Whitham–Richards)

Cars are conserved, so density ρ(x,t) and flow q(ρ) satisfy a conservation law. With Greenshields' speed law v=vf(1−ρ/ρj):

∂ρ∂t+∂q(ρ)∂x=0,q(ρ)=vfρ(1−ρρj)

Information travels at the wave speed q′(ρ)=vf(1−2ρ/ρj), which is negative in dense traffic. Where fast-moving light traffic meets a jam, a shock forms and moves at the Rankine–Hugoniot speed:

s=q(ρL)−q(ρR)ρL−ρR=vf(1−ρL+ρRρj)

During red, arrivals (ρa) meet the stopped jam (ρj): s=−vfρa/ρj=−3.0 m/s. At green an expansion fan opens at the stop line, its back edge moving upstream at −vf. It catches the queue's tail when the queue is longest; with β=ρa/ρj:

t*=R1−β,ℓmax=vfβR1−β,τclear=4β(1−β)R(1−2β)2

Here β=0.2: the queue peaks at 168.8 m at t=56.2 s and has fully dissolved 80 s after green. A numerical (Godunov) solution of the same equation puts the peak queue at 167 m, within 2 m of the formula.

Key findings

  • Jams travel backwards. The queue's back edge is a shock wave moving upstream while every car moves forward or waits: information, not cars, travels at the wave speed.
  • The queue keeps growing after green for 11 s, until the restart wave reaches its back.
  • Short reds need long greens near capacity: clearing takes 80 s of green for a 45 s red at 1,296 veh/h; at 2,025 veh/h (capacity) it would never clear. This cycle needs at least 64% of its time green.
from books.concrete_mathematics import flow as fl
q = fl.signal_queue(v_free=15.0, jam=0.15, arrival_density=0.03, red=45.0)
print(round(q.max_queue, 1), round(q.t_max_queue, 1), round(q.clear_after_green, 1))
print(round(fl.shock_speed(0.03, 0.15, 15.0, 0.15), 3))

Assumptions and extensions

  • Instant acceleration and a smooth fluid. Real discharge has start-up lost time (2–3 s) that the LWR model omits.
  • Greenshields' law gives the parabolic flow curve; a triangular (cell-transmission) curve gives straight-line waves and is standard in traffic software.
  • One lane, no turning. Networks of links and signals are solved with the same Godunov scheme (flow.godunov_traffic).
