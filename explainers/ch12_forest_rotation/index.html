When to cut the forest

Trees keep growing for a century. When should a forester harvest and replant to make the land most valuable?

Chapter 12 · Further optimization. A worked example rebuilt from the chapter's topic in Mike Mesterton-Gibbons, *A Concrete Approach to Mathematical Modelling*; the scenario and numbers are our own illustration, not the book's text.

Headline: At a 3% interest rate, cut every 36 years (Faustmann), not at the 48 years that maximise timber yield. Waiting for maximum yield would cost 10% of the land's value; ignoring the next crop (cutting at 43) costs 4%.

  • 36 yrsFaustmann rotation: maximises land value
  • 48 yrsmaximum-sustained-yield rotation (no discounting)
  • $4,684value of a hectare of bare land under the best rotation

The problem

A hectare of plantation grows timber along an S-shaped curve, levelling off near 500 m³. Timber sells for $50/m³ standing, replanting costs $2,000, and money earns 3% a year. The land will be used for this forever: plant, wait T years, cut, replant, repeat. Which T?

The model

V(T)=Vmax(1−e−kT)m

One rotation is worth (pV(T)−c)e−δT today. An infinite sequence of identical rotations is a geometric series, giving Faustmann's (1849) land expectation value:

LEV(T)=(pV(T)−c)e−δT1−e−δT

Setting its derivative to zero gives the rule: harvest when the value growth of the standing timber falls to the interest on the timber plus the rent on the land it occupies:

pV′(T)=δ(pV(T)−c)+δLEV(T)

Two rival rules drop a term. Fisher's single rotation ignores the land rent (cut later); the forester's maximum sustained yield ignores interest entirely and maximises mean annual increment V(T)/T (cut much later).

Key findings

  • Interest shortens rotations: at 3% the best age is 36 years; at 5% it falls to 31. Capital tied up in standing timber has to earn its keep.
  • The land has a rent: Faustmann cuts 7 years earlier than Fisher's single rotation because delaying the harvest also delays every future crop.
  • Maximum yield is not maximum value: the 48-year rotation grows the most wood per year but is worth 10% less; the two rules coincide only with no interest and no replanting cost.
from books.concrete_mathematics import optimization as op
vol = lambda T: op.chapman_richards(T, v_max=500, k=0.04, m=3)
r = op.faustmann_rotation(vol, price=50, delta=0.03, cost=2000)
print(round(r.faustmann, 1), round(r.fisher, 1), round(r.msy, 1), round(r.lev))

# → 36.0 43.4 47.6 4684

Assumptions and extensions

  • Timber is the only value. Carbon storage, recreation and habitat raise the value of standing trees and lengthen the optimal rotation (Hartman's extension).
  • Fixed prices and growth. Price trends, fire risk (which acts like a higher discount rate) and climate change all shift the answer.
  • Same crop forever. Changing species or land use is a different, larger optimisation.
